# Neighborhood Tool Library (example project) | A shared shed where neighbors borrow tools for free, run by five volunteers. This is a made-up demo map: it shows how an outline is written and what the page looks like.
- Purpose and rules | Why it exists and how it runs. This is the trunk; every other branch serves it. [src: Founding notes]
  - Goal: let neighbors borrow tools instead of buying them [decided] | Started in March by a neighbor who bought a drill and used it twice. Success means a well-stocked shed, not a long member list. [src: Neighborhood chat, 3 March]
    - No membership fees [decided] | Fees would need bookkeeping and insurance; the shed lives on donations.
    - No power tools above 1,500 W [decided] | Higher-powered tools need training the volunteers cannot offer.
  - How lending works
    - Borrow for up to 7 days, renew once [active] | The sign-out sheet by the door is the only record.
      A reminder text goes out on day 8 for anything still missing.
    - Everyone signs a one-page liability waiver [active] | Signed once, kept in the locked box.
    - A damaged tool is repaired or retired on the spot [active]
  - What it will not do [decided]
    - No chainsaws, ladders over 2 m, or anything that needs a licence
- Who runs it | Five volunteers and no paid staff. The church hall provides the space.
  - Founder: coordinates, owns the waiver [active]
  - Volunteer B: repairs and sharpens tools [active]
  - Volunteer C: outreach and the monthly open day [in progress] | Down to weekends only since a job change in September.
  - Volunteer D: keeps the inventory sheet [active]
  - Church hall committee: provides the room and reviews the agreement every year [waiting]
- Inventory and storage [active] | The shed is a back room of the church hall. The details come from branch one of the research.
